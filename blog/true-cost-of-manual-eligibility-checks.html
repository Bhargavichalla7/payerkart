What Manual Insurance Checks Really Cost Your Front Desk

Logging into a payer portal costs nothing. Staying logged in all day to check patients one at a time is where the money goes.

Picture a front-desk coordinator verifying tomorrow's schedule. For each patient she opens the right payer's portal, signs in, waits for a one-time code, types the patient's name and date of birth, reads the benefits screen, and keys the copay and deductible into the EHR. Then she does it again for a patient with a different payer and a different website. None of that shows up as a line on a budget, but it is paid for in salary hours.

Where the minutes go

At the practice where Payerkart is live today, a portal check took about 15 minutes end to end. The time isn't spent reading benefits. It is spent on everything around them:

  • Getting in: separate credentials per payer, and verification codes that arrive when they arrive.
  • Finding the patient: every portal wants identifiers in a slightly different form, and a typo means starting over.
  • Copying results out: benefits are read off one screen and retyped into another, which is also where transcription errors creep in.

Fifteen minutes across 30 patients is seven and a half hours, close to a full shift spent on lookups. The same check, run by an API call from inside the EHR, returns in under a second.

What the industry numbers say

The CAQH Index, an annual benchmark of administrative cost in healthcare, prices the same task by method. In its 2023 edition, a manual eligibility check (portal, phone or fax) costs about $7.97, while a fully electronic one costs about $2.18.

Manual check (portal, phone, fax)$7.97
Fully electronic check$2.18

At 30 checks a day, that difference is roughly $170 a day, or about $3,500 a month for one front desk, before counting denials caused by a check that was skipped or entered wrong.

The portal's price is zero. The check's price is not.

Measure it in your own front desk

You don't have to take a benchmark's word for it. For one week, have staff keep a simple tally: for every eligibility check, note the payer, the minutes it took, and whether it came back clean or needed a second attempt. At the end of the week you will know three things that matter more than any industry average:

  • how many different payer logins your team actually juggles,
  • your real average minutes per check, including the waiting,
  • how often a check fails the first time because of a typo or a missing detail.

Then compare that against your denial reports. If eligibility-related denials show up near the top, the cost of manual checking is already landing on your revenue, not just your payroll.
