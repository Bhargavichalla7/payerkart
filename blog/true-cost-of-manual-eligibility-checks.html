The Real Cost of Checking Eligibility Through the Payer Portal

Payer portals don't charge you to log in. That's why manual eligibility checks rarely show up as a line item anyone questions. But every portal check is paid for in staff time, and that time adds up quickly once you multiply it across every patient on the schedule.

Here's what the industry data says a manual eligibility check actually costs, what it looked like at one practice before and after automating it, and four things you can check in your own workflow this week.

What the CAQH Index says

The CAQH Index is the industry's long-running benchmark for the cost of administrative transactions in US healthcare. Its 2024 report, covering calendar-year 2023, puts eligibility and benefit verification at the top of the volume chart: around 31.5 billion medical eligibility checks in a single year, roughly half of all the medical administrative transactions it tracks.

The cost to a medical provider depends heavily on how each check is done:

  • Fully manual (phone, fax, mail): about $8.57 per check
  • Partially electronic (payer portal or IVR): about $4.46 per check
  • Fully electronic (X12 270/271): about $2.00 per check

CAQH also estimates that moving a single eligibility check from manual to fully electronic saves a provider around 12 minutes of staff time. For specialists and behavioral health providers the gap is wider: in the 2023 Index, they reported spending about 24 minutes on a manual verification.

A portal is "free" in the same way a spreadsheet is free. The cost is the person sitting in front of it.

What it looked like at one practice

At a practice now live on Payerkart, a typical eligibility check through the payer portal took around 15 minutes: log in, find the patient, re-type their details, read the coverage, copy the copay and deductible back into the record. The same check through Payerkart's API returns in under a second, with coverage status, plan details and cost share in the same format for every payer. (Read the case study.)

The math is easy to run for your own front desk. If your team runs 60 manual checks a day at 15 minutes each, that's 15 staff hours a day spent in portals, before anyone has handled a coverage problem.

The costs that don't show up per transaction

The per-check figure is only part of it. Manual checks also create costs that are harder to see:

  • Re-typing errors. A mistyped member ID or date of birth turns into a "no match" in the portal, or worse, a claim rejection weeks later.
  • Stale answers. When checks are expensive, teams run fewer of them. Coverage confirmed at scheduling may have lapsed by the visit.
  • No audit trail. Portal lookups are scattered across logins, so it's hard to show who checked what, and when.
  • Patient wait time. A 15-minute check at the front desk is a 15-minute wait in the lobby.

Four things to check this week

1. Time five portal checks

Ask a front-desk team member to time five routine eligibility checks from login to the answer being in your system. You'll have your own cost per check in an afternoon.

2. Count your daily volume

How many eligibility checks does your team run per day, including re-checks at check-in? Multiply by the time per check to get daily staff hours spent in portals.

3. Find your eligibility-related denials

Pull last month's denials and count the ones caused by inactive coverage, invalid member IDs or demographic mismatches. Those are the denials a better eligibility check would have caught.

4. List the portals your team logs into

Write down every payer portal your staff use for eligibility and claim status. Each one is a separate login, a separate screen layout and a separate place where errors happen.

The takeaway

Manual eligibility checks feel free because nobody gets an invoice for them. The CAQH data and our own practice's experience say otherwise: the real cost is staff time, re-typing errors and denials you only discover after the visit.
